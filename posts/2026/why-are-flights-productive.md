This Thursday, I flew Indigo from Singapore to Chennai. I had no WiFi and got a lot of (slightly difficult) reading done, such as:

- [Machines of Loving Grace](https://tareqistyping.com/prototypes/reading-long-form/)
- [Against the Personal Theory of Agents](https://www.gettheleverage.com/p/against-the-personal-agents-theory)
- Paul Graham's [The Top Idea in Your Mind](https://paulgraham.com/top.html)
- [How I Built the Most AI-Pilled Company in the World](https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826)
- Catching up on [Simon Willison's blog](https://simonwillison.net/)
- Actions from a long pending book cluster summary (The Beginning of Infinity, Conjectures and Refutations, The Logic of Scientific Discovery, The Fabric of Reality, and Enlightenment Now).
- Several tweets, blog posts, and ChatGPT / Claude chats.
- I also drafted prompts to run later.

In a disconnected flight, I'm less distracted because:

- I don't need to check on whether an agent completed.
- I can't open Hacker News if an essay is hard to read.
- I can't click on a link in the essay.

That forces me to absorb _harder_ stuff.

Last Thursday, I flew Emirates from Brussels to Dubai. I had **good (free) WiFi** on the flight. Strangely, I _still_ got a lot of things done:

- [Created an Asterix browser](https://www.s-anand.net/blog/reading-asterix-from-brussels-to-dubai-via-singapore/)
- [Created a comic story using real faces](https://www.s-anand.net/blog/using-real-faces-in-comic-stories/)
- Ran an agent-swarm experiment
- Improved video embedding on my blog
- Published a few blog posts

It's a different _kind_ of productivity - doing rather than understanding - but still felt more productive. I think that's because:

- I didn't have any meetings - unlike typical weekdays.
- I can't snack or watch TV - unlike typical holidays.

I tried this by sitting in libraries, at office on holidays, at cafes and client offices. It's not the same. There's something about the forced "You can't get up for 7 hours" that makes me a bit more productive.

Your mileage may vary. But for me, flights are productive. I thought it was because there was no WiFi but it may be because I can't get up.

A day later, I watched myself as I got distracted. Here's what I noticed.

- When tasks are running in parallel, I get distracted. "What still running? Did it complete?"
  - Grouping all live tasks into a bucket - e.g. a tab group - helps. I'm peaceful there's one place I can go check.
  - Hiding the live task list helps - e.g. the browser vertical tab list and VS Code terminals. It's more work to check what's running.
- When something updates on the screen, I get distracted. "What's that?"
  - Turning off things that update the screen helps, e.g. live sessions, notifications, even the running icons in browser tab / VS Code terminal titles
  - Two screens have more distraction opportunities than one.

When do I get distracted?

- When I get ideas (meetings, reading news), I get distracted. That's a good thing - but I need to complete them and that requires delegation, prioritization, etc.
- After finishing something (submitting a prompt, committing code), I get distracted. That's a good thing - there's no point waiting for an agent to complete, usually.
- While doing other things (exercise, TV, brushing teeth, etc.) I get distracted. Up to a point, that's OK.
- When work is hard (complex, boring), I get distracted. That's a bad thing - and precisely when I need to focus.

This is not structurally different from my [fairly detailed experiments in 2022-23](https://www.s-anand.net/blog/zeigarnik-effect-vs-my-procrastination/). I haven't consciously applied those learnings to AI agents, I guess.

Worth practicing.
