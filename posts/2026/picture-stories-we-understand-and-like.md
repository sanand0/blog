Students of [my course](https://tds.s-anand.net/) were given an [assignment](https://exam.sanand.workers.dev/tds-2026-09-p1#hq-trading-picture-story).

1. Submit a picture story (no words) that others will understand and like.
2. Explain others' picture stories and rank them.

[Here are the results](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/).

Firstly, why this exercise? Because it teaches three skills I believe are important in the AI era:

1. Creating **unique** content
2. Communicating with **clarity**
3. **Marketing** your ideas

This is exactly what they were evaluated on.

1. **Uniqueness**: How similar was their story to others'?
2. **Understanding**: How well did others understand their story?
3. **Preference**: How much did others prefer their story over others'?

One interesting relationship is uniqueness and preference. The two are negatively correlated. I'm not sure why.

[![](https://files.s-anand.net/images/2026-11-07-picture-stories-uniqueness-vs-preference.avif)](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?tab=patterns).

The least unique stories were about these three patterns - and there were _so many_ of them!

- [A crow drinking water from a pitcher](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-asc#story=P124)
- [A child adopting a dog/bird](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-asc#story=P006)
- [An older man helping a child with a kite](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-asc#story=P012)
- [A child/robot watering a plant](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-asc#story=P337)

If people can't come up with original stories easily, I guess there's a concrete skill to learn _and_ a way to differentiate capability.

The most unique stories _were_ reasonably interesting. They included:

- [An unwanted gift](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-desc#story=P292)
- [Cell notifications at the moon landing](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-desc#story=P133)
- [The growth of AI](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-desc#story=P295)
- [Robots replacing humans](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=uniqueness-desc#story=P260)

The top scoring story was fairly unique, too: [The Bhagavad Gita](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/#story=P253).

I was surprised at how interesting some of the _low_ scoring stories were. For example, I thought this [photographer deletes a baby panda photo to protect it](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=understanding-asc#story=P089) was really powerful.

[![](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/gallery/P089.webp)](https://files.s-anand.net/pages/tds-2026-09-p1-q7-picture-stories/?sort=understanding-asc#story=P089)

It ranked low on understanding (3 people completely mis-understood the story but since they labelled the story "A thirsty crow" or "A steam engine..." I think these were errors or junk). It ranked only ranked mid-way on preference.

So, maybe there's more to learn than what I was measuring.

Still, it was good to know that we can teach and evaluate useful skills even when students can use AI and collaborate.
