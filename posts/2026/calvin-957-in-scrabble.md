On 28 Feb 1990, Calvin and Hobbes played Scrabble.

![](https://picayune.uclick.com/comics/ch/1990/ch900228.gif)

**Calvin**: Ha! I've got a great word and it's on a "double word score" box!\
**Hobbes**: "ZQFMGB" isn't a word! It doesn't even have a vowel!\
**Calvin**: It is SO a word! It's worm found in new guinea! Everyone knows that!\
**Hobbes**: I'm looking it up.\
**Calvin**: You do, and I'll look up that 12-letter word YOU played with all the Xs and Js!\
**Hobbes**: What's your score for ZQFMGB?\
**Calvin**: 957.

Question is, how do you score 957 on a double-word score? It isn't even _even_!

But, it turns out, it's possible - even in a realistic game of Scrabble, with standard rules, a standard board, but lots of tiles, and an increasingly creative dictionary.

Claude recreated the game these two would have played, descending from a perfectly legitimate game into a mess of Calvinball, reproducing the "12-letter word YOU played with all the Xs and Js" and ending with _exactly_ 957 points for "ZQFMGB". Here it is!
