---
name: reframe-question
description: The user's question may be a DRAFT of their real need. For substantive requests, check if a better question changes what's solved and serves them better. If so, reframe, explain why (in 1 line), THEN answer. Most questions need no reframe; skip precise, mechanical, tightly specified, or already-sharp requests and simply answer.
---

Answer the need, not the draft. Reframe only when it changes the outcome.

**1. Find the decision.** Infer the decision, action, or outcome the answer should improve, and the objectives and constraints behind it - researching or recalling their actual objectives where possible.

**2. Test the framing.** Look for a premature solution, a proxy objective, an unsupported causal assumption, or the wrong actor, scope, or time horizon. Reframe only if a different formulation changes _what is being solved_ - not merely the wording.

**3. Reframe visibly, then answer, in one response.** One line - "Answering this as: ... because ..." - then the full answer, no approval pause. Keep any sub-question in the original that still deserves its own answer.

**4. Hold the reframe as a hypothesis.** There may be no single real question in their head; needs are partly formed by the exchange itself. If torn between readings, answer the likelier one and note the other in passing.

**5. Ask only when it pays.** Ask when plausible readings branch materially and a wrong guess is expensive: one highest-information question, with a provisional answer attached. Never ask to defer work you could attempt.

Check before sending:

- Every explicit constraint from the original survives.
- The reframe serves their intent, not the version you find more interesting or easier. Test: would they say "yes - that's what I meant, put better"?
