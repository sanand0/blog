# Embedding Analysis

This analyzes `analysis/embeddings.parquet`, generated by `scripts/embeddings.py`, and joins it with front matter from `posts/**/*.md` and `pages/**/*.md`.

The analysis script is [`analyze_embeddings.py`](./analyze_embeddings.py). It writes plots, summary CSVs/Parquet files, and this README into the same folder.

## TL;DR

- The embeddings are useful. For labeled posts, the nearest neighbor shares a category **84.6%** of the time, versus a **46.5%** random baseline. Even with `links` dominating the corpus, that is still a **1.82x lift**. See [`category-alignment.csv`](./category-alignment.csv).
- The current taxonomy is looser than the embedding space. `links` has **1,791 posts** and gets split across at least **7** fine-grained clusters. Meanwhile `linkedin`, `llms`, `coding`, and `how-i-do-things` collapse into a much tighter modern AI/workflow neighborhood.
- The space clearly captures a shift in your writing over time. The biggest yearly centroid moves are **2020** and **2021**, and by **2025-2026** almost all posts sit in one broad semantic phase.
- Pages are semantically useful, but long pages are underrepresented by the current embedding pipeline. **39.7% of pages** were truncated at 10k chars, including **77.8% of `pages/notes`**.
- For related-post suggestions, plain cosine neighbors are already good. A production version should add a small hub penalty, MMR-style diversification, and cleaner inputs (strip comments, chunk long pages).

## Corpus And Caveats

- Documents analyzed: **2,741**
- Posts: **2,673**
- Pages: **68**
- Documents with front-matter categories: **2,670**
- Truncated by the 10k-char cap: **107**
- Documents with embedded comments sections: **594**

Important caveat: these embeddings are not "pure article embeddings". `scripts/embeddings.py` embeds:

- `title + body`
- front matter stripped
- **comments included**
- first **10,000 chars only**

That matters. A few documents are nearly all comments, and some long `notes` / `lists` pages are represented mostly by their intros.

## 1. Do The Embeddings Capture Categories?

Yes, mostly.

For posts with categories, nearest-neighbor agreement is strong:

| k | same-category hit rate | baseline | lift |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 84.6% | 46.5% | 1.82x |
| 3 | 83.2% | 46.5% | 1.79x |
| 5 | 82.2% | 46.5% | 1.77x |
| 10 | 80.3% | 46.5% | 1.73x |

Source: [`category-alignment.csv`](./category-alignment.csv)

The most semantically tight existing categories are:

- `quizzes`: within-category similarity **0.807**, or **1.35x** the corpus average
- `london-2000`: **1.17x**
- `london-2005`: **1.15x**
- `llms`: **1.13x**
- `linkedin`: **1.12x**

Source: [`category-cohesion.csv`](./category-cohesion.csv)

What the embeddings do **not** capture cleanly is the current category system as a one-cluster-per-category taxonomy:

- Fine-cluster/category NMI is only **0.310**. Broad-cluster/category NMI is **0.365**.
- `links` alone has **1,791 posts**, but it spreads across fine clusters `0,1,3,4,5,6,7`.
- All **114 `linkedin` posts** land in fine cluster **2**.
- `llms` splits mostly into fine clusters **2** and **10** (`50` + `24` posts).
- `coding` splits across clusters **9**, **2**, and **10**.
- `how-i-do-things` splits across clusters **8**, **2**, **9**, and **10**.

This suggests the embeddings are capturing something more coherent than the taxonomy: topic + format + era + mode of thinking.

## 2. What Clusters Show That Categories Miss

- **4 broad clusters** for macro phases of the corpus
- **12 fine clusters** for actual archetypes

The broad clusters are highly interpretable:

- Broad cluster `0`: early web/search/link-blog material
- Broad cluster `1`: link-heavy but more personal/blog-product/news commentary
- Broad cluster `3`: mid-period personal/music/quizzes/consumer topics
- Broad cluster `2`: recent AI/coding/workshop/notes era

## 5. Pages Vs Posts

Pages are not isolated from posts in embedding space:

- A page's top-5 neighbors are **56.8% posts** and **43.2% pages**
- A post's top-5 neighbors are only **0.57% pages**
- **76.5% of all pages** sit in fine clusters **2** and **10**, i.e. the recent AI/workflow part of the space

This is a good asymmetry:

- pages can be used as semantic "maps" into posts
- post-to-post recommendation will not be overwhelmed by pages

The strongest page-section patterns:

- `prompts`: **52.7%** of top-5 neighbors are posts
- `notes`: **46.7%** are posts
- `lists`: **80.0%** are posts
- `quotes`: mostly page-to-page

Sources:

- [`kind-neighbor-mix.csv`](./kind-neighbor-mix.csv)
- [`documents.csv`](./documents.csv)

Some nice cross-kind examples:

- `pages/prompts/fake-data.md` links to `How to Fake Data That Tells a Story`, `Benford Law`, and `Vibe-Coding for Interesting Data Stories`
- `pages/notes/gemini-tqdm-rich-task-id-bug.md` links to `Finding open source bugs with Ty`, `Turning Walks into Pull Requests`, and `Mistakes AI Coding Agents Make`
- `pages/lists/book-clusters.md` links to `I count AI summarized books as "Read"` and the `Books in 2021/2022/2023/2025` posts

So yes: embedding space can surface meaningful relationships between pages and posts.

## 6. Can You Use This For Better Related-Post Suggestions?

Yes.

The existing nearest-neighbor quality is already good enough to ship a first version:

- `Voice Chat to Slides: My New AI-Powered Workflow` gets neighbors like `Tools to publish annotated talks from videos`, `AfterSlides`, `Voice coding is the new live coding`, and `Command Line Slideshows in Bash`
- `The most popular scientific Python modules` gets neighbors like `Automating PowerPoint with Python`, `Client side scraping`, and `To Python from Perl`

The more interesting result is that the space can bridge across category and even across content type:

- `Realistic fake data generator` finds both recent AI/data-story posts and `Benford Law` from 2005
- `book-clusters` finds the book-review / reading-history posts even though it is a page, not a post

I exported two recommendation strategies in [`related-posts.parquet`](./related-posts.parquet):

- `pure_cosine`
- `diversified_mmr`

The MMR rerank is a small improvement when the cosine list is too hub-heavy:

- Mean rank-1 similarity stays identical at **0.794**
- Same-category rate at rank 1 stays identical at **80.7%**
- Mean top-5 similarity barely changes: **0.7682 -> 0.7673**
- But diversity improves:
  - distinct target clusters per source: **2.139 -> 2.257**
  - distinct target categories per source: **1.642 -> 1.703**

Example:

- For `The Surprising Power of LLMs: Jack-of-All-Trades`, pure cosine returns `Workshops That Teach Me More Than You` (hubness **56**).
- The diversified rerank swaps that out for `my top chatgpt chat categories`, which is slightly less obvious and more varied while staying semantically close.

My recommendation for production related posts:

1. Restrict candidates to `kind='post'`
2. Start from top-15 cosine neighbors
3. Penalize high-hubness posts slightly
4. Rerank to top-5 using MMR or a similar diversity heuristic
5. Optionally add a small boost for shared category/tag, but do not require it

## 7. What Categories Or Tags Should Change?

### High-confidence changes

1. Break up `links`

`links` is the biggest mismatch between taxonomy and semantic space. It behaves like a catch-all bucket for multiple unrelated archetypes.

Suggested replacements:

- `web-search-and-blogging`
- `internet-culture-and-oddities`
- `science-and-ideas`
- `india-business-and-telecom`
- `tools-and-howto-links`

If you do not want more categories, at least add these as tags and keep `links` only as a legacy label.

2. Add an umbrella for the modern AI/workflow corpus

The current space says `llms`, `linkedin`, parts of `coding`, and parts of `how-i-do-things` belong to one newer family.

Suggested new category or top-level tag:

- `ai-workflows`

Suggested sub-tags:

- `agent-engineering`
- `prompt-patterns`
- `vibe-coding`
- `ai-writing`
- `workshops-and-talks`
- `tooling-and-evals`

3. Treat `funny` more like a tone tag than a topic category

`funny` is only **1.02x** as cohesive as the corpus average. Its neighbors spill into `how-i-do-things`, `llms`, and `my-best-links`. It feels more like "how this post sounds" than "what this post is about".

4. Consider turning `my-best-links` into a flag/tag instead of a semantic category

It mostly overlaps with `links`, but acts more like an editorial marker than a separate topic.

### Categories That Already Work Well

- `quizzes`
- `llms`
- `linkedin`
- `london-2000`
- `london-2005`

These have strong internal cohesion and can stay.

## 8. Pipeline Improvements I Would Make Before Re-Embedding

The current embeddings are good enough to analyze, but not yet ideal for production navigation.

I would change the embedding input like this:

1. Strip the `## Comments` section before embedding
2. Remove long image/link dumps where the article body is tiny
3. Chunk long pages and notes instead of truncating at 10k chars
4. Store one document embedding plus optional chunk embeddings
5. Keep title in the input, but maybe separate title/body embeddings if you want more control

Why this matters:

- **39.7% of pages** are truncated
- **77.8% of `notes` pages** are truncated
- **66.7% of `lists` pages** are truncated
- Some documents are **>90% comments**

Truncated documents are actually a bit *more* locally cohesive than non-truncated ones (`0.792` vs `0.767` mean top-5 similarity), which is a sign that the intro-heavy 10k cap may be collapsing long pages into a common style/format neighborhood.
